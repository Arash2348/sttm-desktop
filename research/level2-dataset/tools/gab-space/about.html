What this is

Voice-Follow listens to kirtan or paath and puts the pangti being sung or read on the screen, so the sangat can follow. GAB (Gurbani Autopilot Bench) plays the same recordings to every version of the app and counts how well it did. Each row of the leaderboard is one version ("build"). The recordings are replayed from saved speech-model output, so the same build always gets exactly the same numbers.

The numbers are split by what is happening, because each is a different job:

Kirtan of a Shabad (ragis singing a Shabad), Bani recited (paath of Japji, Jaap, Tav Prasad Savaiye, Chaupai, Anand Sahib, Rehras, Sohila, Saloks), Bani sung like kirtan (Aarti, Asa di Vaar, Rehras or Saloks sung), and Akhand Paath (Guru Granth Sahib Ji read Ang after Ang). Akhand Paath has no test runner yet, so it shows no numbers.

The columns

Right Shabad (right Shabad or Bani on screen; higher is better). Of all the seconds Gurbani is being sung or read, the share where the screen shows that Shabad or Bani (any line of it). The answer key is made by people for every recording.

Right pangti (the sangat sees the right pangti; higher is better). The share of the seconds where the exact pangti being sung is on screen, or the app is less than 1 second into switching to it. This needs an answer key for every line, which we only have for some recordings, so under the number it says how many clips it rests on and who made the answer key: human answers (the sevadaar's own screen, or a person), aligner, ear-checked (made by our program, then checked by ear), or aligner only (made by our program and not checked; shown, but not trusted as a headline). Recordings where the app never showed anything are counted apart ("never shown"), because that loss is already in the first column.

Behind (seconds behind; lower is better). When a new pangti starts, how many seconds until it is on screen. The big number is the typical (middle) value: half are faster. "Worst 10%", on the small line, is the value only the slowest tenth are above.

Wrong Shabad (seconds of wrong Shabad per hour; lower is better). How long the screen shows a different Shabad or Bani from the one being sung, per hour of singing. 36 seconds per hour is 1% of the time.

Find (seconds to find; lower is better). The first Shabad or Bani of each recording: how long until it is on screen. Most recordings were cut from the middle of a program, so this is timed from the moment the app starts listening ("cold start", like a sevadaar pressing Start in the middle of a Shabad). n is how many such starts there are; "never found" counts the ones that never appeared. The typical and worst-10% times are worked out only over the ones that were found, so always read them together with "never found".

Switch (three counts: found within 10 s / found later / never found). When the ragis move on to a different Shabad (or Aarti ends and the next Shabad begins), how long until the new one is on screen, timed from its first words. It only counts when the screen was showing something else as the new Shabad began. The small line gives the typical time over the switches that were found (so always read it with "never"), and two more counts. Stayed on main Shabad: the ragis quote another Shabad briefly (a parmaan) and come back, and the app never left the main Shabad; that is correct, and it is not counted as a switch. Parmaan shown: how many of those quoted Shabads the app put on screen.

thin means fewer than 3 recordings or events are behind that number: treat it as an anecdote, not a score.

Which recordings, and what is held back

Every recording session is put once, by a fixed rule, into a practice set (train and dev) or a held-out test set. This leaderboard only shows the practice set. The test set is kept for a final check before a release, so that we never tune the app to the exact recordings we judge it on.

Some numbers we reported before (for example the Renton Rehras and Sohila being about 0.4 s behind the reader) were measured on recordings that are now in the test set, so they cannot appear here. The tab "Old numbers, checked" lists each number we reported, where it came from, and whether GAB gets the same number on the same recordings.

Mixer feed or room microphone matters a lot: the Fremont recordings made with STTM Desktop in the hall are much harder than livestream audio. We assume those were made with a laptop microphone and that the livestreams carry the mixer feed; neither is confirmed yet. Kirtan line numbers here come only from those Fremont hall recordings.

Builds

merged = in Khalis Foundation's experimental-release branch. candidate = a proposed change, tested here before it is merged.

PASS / FAIL next to each build: the build against the one before it, with the same guardrails as gab compare. A candidate is compared with the build it was made from; a merged build with the merged build before it. FAIL means at least one behaviour got worse beyond a small tolerance (on all its clips, or on the mixer-feed or room-mic clips alone); the list under the leaderboard says which numbers. A FAIL is not always a reason to reject a change (fast Aarti traded more wrong-Shabad time for speed on purpose), but it must be seen and decided, never hidden.

Source and the full per-clip scorecards: the dataset khalisfoundation/GurbaniAutopilotBench (private) and the tools research/level2-dataset/tools/GAB.md in sttm-replay.
