What this is

Voice-Follow listens to kirtan or paath and puts the pangti being sung or read on the screen, so the sangat can follow along. GAB (Gurbani Autopilot Bench) plays the same recordings to every version of the app and counts how well it did. Each row of the leaderboard is one version (a "build"). The recordings are replayed from saved speech-model output, so the same build always gets exactly the same numbers.

The numbers are split by what is happening, because each is a different job:

Kirtan of a Shabad (ragis singing a Shabad), Bani recited (paath of Japji Sahib, Jaap Sahib, Tav Prasad Savaiye, Chaupai Sahib, Anand Sahib, Rehras Sahib, Sohila, Saloks), Bani sung like kirtan (Aarti, Asa di Vaar, Rehras Sahib or Saloks sung), and Akhand Paath (Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji read Ang after Ang). Akhand Paath has no test runner yet, so it shows no numbers.

Two leaderboards: GAB and GAB-Hard

GAB (the main one, picked at the top of the page) is what we judge every build on. It has every practice recording except one group.

GAB-Hard (Fremont hall, laptop mic) is that group: Jaspal's recordings at Gurdwara Sahib Fremont, made with STTM Desktop on the sevadaar's laptop. The hall echoes a lot, the laptop mic is far from the ragis, and there is not much clean audio, so these are very hard to follow. We keep scoring them, on their own, so we can see progress there, but they are not the priority, and a build is never passed or failed because of them. The two are never mixed into one number: each has its own PASS / FAIL against the build before it.

The Fremont livestream clips (the YouTube broadcast, which comes from the mixer, not a mic in the hall) are ordinary recordings and stay in main GAB.

The columns

Right Shabad (right Shabad or Bani on screen; higher is better). Of all the seconds Gurbani is being sung or read, the share where the screen shows that Shabad or Bani (any pangti of it). A person made the answer key for every recording.

Right pangti (strict; higher is better). The share of the seconds where the exact pangti being sung is on screen, or the app is less than 1 second into moving to it. This needs an answer key for every pangti, made by a person (on GAB-Hard: the sevadaar's own screen, which may lag a little). Under the number it says how many recordings it rests on. Where we only have a few recordings with such answers, or only answers made by our own program and never checked, the cell says no line answers yet instead of showing a number that could mislead. Main GAB kirtan is like that today: for main kirtan we only know whether the right Shabad was on screen. Recordings where the app never showed anything are counted apart ("never shown"), because that loss is already in Right Shabad.

Same or neighbouring pangti (higher is better). Like Right pangti, but it also counts the time the screen shows the pangti just before or just after the one being sung. In kirtan the ragi often sings two pangtis as one phrase, while the answer key splits them, so the partner pangti gets marked wrong even though the sangat is reading along fine. Read the two columns together: strict says "exactly right", neighbouring says "close enough to follow".

Behind (seconds behind; lower is better). When a new pangti starts, how many seconds until it is on screen. The big number is the typical (middle) value: half are faster. "Worst 10%", on the small line, is the value only the slowest tenth are above.

Wrong Shabad (seconds of wrong Shabad per hour; lower is better). How long the screen shows a different Shabad or Bani from the one being sung, per hour of singing. 36 seconds per hour is 1% of the time.

Find (seconds to find; lower is better). The first Shabad or Bani of each recording: how long until it is on screen. Most recordings were cut from the middle of a program, so this is timed from the moment the app starts listening ("cold start", like a sevadaar pressing Start in the middle of a Shabad). n is how many such starts there are; "never found" counts the ones that never appeared. The typical and worst-10% times are worked out only over the ones that were found, so always read them together with "never found".

Switch (three counts: found within 10 s / found later / never found). When the ragis move on to a different Shabad (or Aarti ends and the next Shabad begins), how long until the new one is on screen, timed from its first words. It only counts when the screen was showing something else as the new Shabad began. The small line gives the typical time over the switches that were found (so always read it with "never"), and two more counts. Stayed on main Shabad: the ragis quote another Shabad briefly (a parmaan) and come back, and the app never left the main Shabad; that is correct, and it is not counted as a switch. Parmaan shown: how many of those quoted Shabads the app put on screen.

thin means fewer than 3 recordings or events are behind that number: treat it as an anecdote, not a score.

Which recordings, and what is held back

Every recording session is put once, by a fixed rule, into a practice set (train and dev) or a held-out test set. This leaderboard only shows the practice set, for both GAB and GAB-Hard. The test set is kept for a final check before a release, so that we never tune the app to the exact recordings we judge it on.

Some numbers we reported before (for example the Renton Rehras Sahib and Sohila being about 0.4 s behind the reader) were measured on recordings that are now in the test set, so they cannot appear here. The tab "Old numbers, checked" lists each number we reported, where it came from, and whether GAB gets the same number on the same recordings; the Fremont hall rows are listed when GAB-Hard is picked.

Mixer feed or room microphone matters a lot. We assume the Fremont hall recordings were made with a laptop microphone and that the livestreams carry the mixer feed; neither is confirmed yet.

Builds

merged = in Khalis Foundation's experimental-release branch. candidate = a proposed change, tested here before it is merged.

PASS / FAIL next to each build: the build against the one before it, with the same guardrails as gab compare, worked out separately for GAB and for GAB-Hard. A candidate is compared with the build it was made from; a merged build with the merged build before it. FAIL means at least one behaviour got worse beyond a small tolerance (on all its clips, or on the mixer-feed or room-mic clips alone); the list under the leaderboard says which numbers. Pangti numbers that the table does not show (too few or unchecked line answers) never cause a FAIL. A FAIL can be a deliberate trade-off, but it must be seen and decided, never hidden.
