What this is

Voice-Follow listens to kirtan or paath and puts the pangti being sung or read on the screen, so the sangat can follow. GAB (Gurbani Autopilot Bench) plays the same recordings to every version of the app and counts how well it did. Each row of the leaderboard is one version ("build"). The recordings are replayed from saved speech-model output, so the same build always gets exactly the same numbers.

The numbers are split by what is happening, because each is a different job:

Kirtan of a Shabad (ragis singing a Shabad), Bani recited (paath of Japji, Jaap, Tav Prasad Savaiye, Chaupai, Anand Sahib, Rehras, Sohila, Saloks), Bani sung like kirtan (Aarti, Asa di Vaar, Rehras or Saloks sung), and Akhand Paath (Guru Granth Sahib Ji read Ang after Ang). Akhand Paath has no test runner yet, so it shows no numbers.

The columns

Right Shabad or Bani on screen (higher is better). Of all the seconds Gurbani is being sung or read, the share where the screen shows that Shabad or Bani (any line of it). The answer key is made by people for every recording.

Sangat sees the right pangti (higher is better). The share of the seconds where the exact pangti being sung is on screen, or the app is less than 1 second into switching to it. This needs an answer key for every line, which we only have for some recordings, so under the number it says how many clips it rests on and who made the answer key: human answers (the sevadaar's own screen, or a person), aligner, ear-checked (made by our program, then checked by ear), or aligner only (made by our program and not checked; shown, but not trusted as a headline). Recordings where the app never showed anything are counted apart ("never shown"), because that loss is already in the first column.

Seconds behind (lower is better). When a new pangti starts, how many seconds until it is on screen. "Typical" is the middle value (half are faster); "worst 10%" is the value only the slowest tenth are above.

Seconds of wrong Shabad per hour (lower is better). How long the screen shows a different Shabad or Bani from the one being sung, per hour of singing. 36 seconds per hour is 1% of the time.

Seconds to find (lower is better). The first Shabad or Bani of each recording: how long until it is on screen. Most recordings were cut from the middle of a program, so this is timed from the moment the app starts listening ("cold start", like a sevadaar pressing Start in the middle of a Shabad). n is how many such starts there are; "never found" counts the ones that never appeared.

Seconds to switch (lower is better). When the ragis move on to a different Shabad (or Aarti ends and the next Shabad begins), how long until the new one is on screen, timed from its first words.

thin means fewer than 3 recordings or events are behind that number: treat it as an anecdote, not a score.

Which recordings, and what is held back

Every recording session is put once, by a fixed rule, into a practice set (train and dev) or a held-out test set. This leaderboard only shows the practice set. The test set is kept for a final check before a release, so that we never tune the app to the exact recordings we judge it on.

Some numbers we reported before (for example the Renton Rehras and Sohila being about 0.4 s behind the reader) were measured on recordings that are now in the test set, so they cannot appear here. The tab "Old numbers, checked" lists each number we reported, where it came from, and whether GAB gets the same number on the same recordings.

Mixer feed or room microphone matters a lot: Fremont recordings made with a laptop in the hall are much harder than livestream (mixer) audio. Kirtan line numbers here come only from those Fremont laptop recordings.
